Fivestarman 45-Day Challenge

Week Five

Discussion Guide · Days 29 through 35

Guide
A word before we start

Week Five is character under load. Days 29-35 run from the words you let out of your mouth, to giving yourself away, to being coached and becoming the coach, to selling without losing your integrity, to the way social media exposes what's already in you, to standing firm when the enemy gambles for your identity, and finally to the cause you'd pour everything into if money were no object. This is the stretch where what a man says, gives, and stands on gets tested in the open.

Day 29 · Wednesday June 3

Watch Over Your Words

Faithful
Summary

Day 29 calls a man to take his speech seriously, because exaggeration and careless overspeak create gaps where the enemy works and where you unknowingly pronounce lack, sickness, or defeat over your own life. Between a clear yes and a clear no sits a gray area, and that gap is exactly where the nemesis of man looks for an opening. The fix is plain, honest, measured speech: say what you mean and mean what you say, replacing dramatic negativity with simple, even positive, statements that carry more weight precisely because they are not inflated.

Verse

Simply let your Yes be Yes, and your No, No; anything beyond this comes from the evil one.

Matthew 5:37
Discussion Question

What are some overspeak phrases you catch yourself using regularly, and what do you think they reveal about where your mind and heart actually are?

  • Pronouncements of lack like I'm broke or I'll never afford it that quietly shape how you see your finances
  • Pronouncements over your body like I'm sick and tired or that's killing me
  • The dramatic highs and lows that come out under stress at work or in a tense moment at home
  • Catching exaggeration in front of your kids, who learn to speak the way you speak
  • Replacing the inflated statement with a measured one, and noticing it actually lands harder
  • The link between loose speech and a weak prayer life: words you don't govern undercut the words you pray
  • Mending the hedge so there's no gap left open between your yes and your no
Day 30 · Thursday June 4

Blessed To Give

Philanthropic
Summary

Day 30 reframes giving not as obligation but as identity: to be a man made in God's image is to be a giver, and the philanthropic passion is the deliberate counterweight to the greed that the entrepreneurial drive can breed. Kennedy names six ways to give yourself away, money, time, expertise, love, random acts of kindness, and appreciation, and points to research that spending on others raises happiness while spending on yourself does not. The challenge is concrete and immediate: pick from those six and actually give yourself away today. You're blessed to be a blessing.

Verse

It is more blessed to give than to receive.

Acts 20:35
Discussion Question

Of the five ways to give yourself away, financially, time, expertise, love, acts of kindness, appreciation, which comes most naturally to you, and which do you most need to grow in?

  • Financial giving that stays steady regardless of what the bank account looks like that month
  • Time as the hardest one to give because it's the most limited and the most demanded
  • Expertise: using the exact skill set you get paid for to serve someone who can't pay
  • Love and appreciation that go unspoken at home because you assume they're already known
  • Naming the one you skip, not the one you wish were hardest
  • Random acts of kindness as the easiest entry point when the others feel stretched thin
  • Giving as a defense against the greed that quietly grows alongside ambition
Day 31 · Friday June 5

Be A Mentor

Adventurous
Summary

Kennedy frames manhood through the coach and player relationship: every man, no matter how skilled, needs someone to draw out what's in him and sharpen what's dull, because even champions need a coach. But the lesson runs both directions. The man being coached eventually becomes the coach. To be a Fivestarman is to take responsibility for drafting younger men to new levels, translating hard won skill on the field of contest into the field of life so others can win at it too. The paired reading sits in deliberate tension: a mentor sharpens you, but there's one place no coach can follow, the solitary wrestling match with God where the blessing is seized alone.

Verse

These things happened to them as examples and were written down as warnings for us.

1 Corinthians 10:11
Discussion Question

Who has been the most significant coach or mentor in your life, and what specific quality in them do you most want to pass on to someone else?

  • A coach, boss, father, or older man from your church who drew something out of you that you couldn't see yourself
  • The specific trait worth passing on: their patience, their standards, the way they told you the truth
  • A younger man already in your orbit who needs what you've got, whether you've stepped into it or not
  • The honest question of whether you currently have a coach, or whether you've quietly outgrown being teachable
  • The difference between giving advice and actually conditioning someone for the game of life
  • The fight no mentor can enter for you, the solitary reckoning with God you may be avoiding
  • Wounds from a friend that can be trusted: the value of someone who'll counter your moves, not just affirm them
Day 32 · Saturday June 6

The Art Of Salesmanship

Entrepreneurial
Summary

Day 32 makes the case that basic sales ability is a skill every man needs, because persuasion is woven through business, marriage, parenting, and faith. Kennedy lays out the keys to closing a deal that's really a code for persuading anyone toward something good: listen without interrupting, acknowledge objections before you resolve them, stay pleasant rather than argumentative, don't assume the other person's knowledge or need, and make sure every transaction is a genuine win for both sides. The paired reading on the Law of Exchange grounds it deeper, all value transfer requires release, the same principle that runs through labor, tithing, and God giving His Son to receive sons.

Verse

A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger.

Proverbs 15:1
Discussion Question

Whether in business, marriage, parenting, or faith, where in your life do you most need to improve your ability to persuade others toward something good?

  • The sales key you violate most: interrupting, getting argumentative, or assuming you already know their need
  • Persuasion in marriage, bringing your spouse toward a decision without steamrolling or going silent
  • Parenting as the long sell, winning your kids' hearts toward what's good rather than just demanding it
  • Sharing your faith without slipping into a pitch, listening first so you actually meet the person
  • Making sure the transaction is a real win for the other side, not just a win you dressed up as theirs
  • Acknowledging the objection out loud before you try to answer it
  • The exchange underneath it all: nothing of value transfers without something being released first
Day 33 · Sunday June 7

The Friend Virus

Gallant
Summary

Day 33 frames social media not as the source of bad behavior but as a mirror that exposes what's already in a man, and warns that overexposure to godless chatter, empty, vain, useless talk, pulls a man steadily toward ungodliness. The lesson offers a practical set of guardrails: protect your privacy, avoid drama and TMI, never type what you wouldn't say to someone's face, steer clear of private chats with the opposite sex, and be willing to walk away entirely if it's costing you. The bottom line is stewardship, everything you post is public and permanent, so conduct yourself accordingly.

Verse

Avoid godless chatter, because those who indulge in it will become more and more ungodly.

2 Timothy 2:16
Discussion Question

If you're honest, how much of your time on social media actually builds you up or builds others up, and what would it look like to set a healthier boundary with it?

  • What your feed and your posting habits actually expose about where your attention and heart are
  • Time lost to scrolling that you'd never have agreed to spend if someone had asked for it up front
  • The drama and outrage you get pulled into that leaves you more agitated, not more godly
  • Private messaging that drifts past what you'd be comfortable saying face to face
  • A concrete boundary: time limits, deleting an app, or leaving a platform entirely like some men already have
  • The reminder that it's all public record, which alone would change half of what gets posted
  • Using the tool for genuine connection and encouragement instead of letting it use you
Day 34 · Monday June 8

The Devil Is A Gambler

Faithful
Summary

The devotion frames spiritual warfare in gambler's terms: the enemy is betting you'll forfeit something of value, but Scripture says a thief caught must repay double, so what's being attacked is actually positioned to come back to you greater if you hold the line. Kennedy warns against two opposite errors, underestimating the enemy's deception or overestimating his power, both of which hand him the advantage. The path to winning is drawn straight from Christ's wilderness temptation across three fronts: know your identity and don't trade who you are for what you want, keep your footing and walk ordered steps, and stay focused by refusing the shortcut that skips the sacrifice. Stand firm without compromise and the enemy flees.

Verse

The thief, if he is caught, must pay back double.

Exodus 22:7
Discussion Question

Which of the three tactics Jesus used in the wilderness, knowing who you are, keeping your footing, or staying focused, do you feel most vulnerable to losing ground in right now?

  • Identity: the bluff of if you are who you say you are, trading who you are for a quick win or approval
  • Footing: reckless steps, cutting corners, or letting one area slide until it trips the rest up
  • Focus: the offer of the whole world without the sacrifice, the shortcut that always costs more later
  • Where in business or at home the enemy is currently betting you'll fold
  • Naming whether you're underestimating the deception or overestimating the threat, since both lose ground
  • The promise that what's under attack is positioned to be returned double if you don't waver
  • Standing firm in integrity as the move that actually makes the enemy flee
Day 35 · Tuesday June 9

A Cause Worth Your Resources

Philanthropic
Summary

Day 35 turns the philanthropic passion from a feeling into a target. A man's resources, his money, time, skill, and influence, always flow somewhere, and the question is whether they flow toward a cause that outlasts him or just toward his own comfort. Kennedy presses past the daydream of if money were no object to the real point: the cause you'd pour everything into if you could is usually the cause God has already wired you to care about, and you don't have to wait for unlimited resources to take the first step toward it. Generosity becomes legacy when it's aimed, not just felt.

Verse

For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.

Matthew 6:21
Discussion Question

If money were no object, what cause or need would you most passionately pour your resources into, and what small step could you take toward that today?

  • The cause that already pulls at you, children in poverty, the fatherless, disaster relief, the local church
  • Noticing that what you'd fund with unlimited money points to what God already put on your heart
  • A small step available now: a recurring gift, a day of service, lending your skill set to the work
  • The difference between admiring a cause from a distance and actually aiming resources at it
  • Giving through time and expertise in the season when the dollars are tight
  • Bringing your family into the cause so generosity becomes something your kids inherit
  • Treating this as the start of a legacy worth leaving, not a someday daydream

How can we pray for you this week?
